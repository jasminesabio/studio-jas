01

Music & workflow

Afterglow

Live demo → GitHub → Hackathon page →

DJ sets live or die by preparation. Knowing what you have, how it feels, and what comes next. But most artists organize their music in folders, which only tells you one thing: genre. Nothing about energy, arc, or where a track belongs in a night.

Afterglow is a track indexing tool that adds the dimensions folders can't: energy level, time of day, part of set (warm up, build, decompress, closing), and mood tags. Built to make it faster to pull the right track under pressure and plan sets with more intention.

This came directly from my own DJ practice. Hear what I play →

02

Event & logistics

Wedding Website

My husband and I built two separate sites for our wedding: one for guests, one for our planners. The information each audience needed was completely different, so keeping it together would have served neither well.

The guest site had everything for the full weekend: schedule, venue details, hotel blocks, local recommendations. The planner site was an ops layer: guest info, transportation logistics, seating assignments, and a minute-by-minute run of show that people could actually find and trust.

  • Multi-audience design
  • Event operations
  • Information hierarchy
  • Logistics UX
  • Real-time coordination

03

Learning & resources

Climate Intelligence Hub

Visit site →

A self-directed learning platform for people trying to break into climate tech and clean energy. I built it for myself, studying the industry at my own pace using free online materials, and opened it up because plenty of others are doing the same.

Covers 13 topics from foundations to sector deep dives: energy and grid, climate finance, carbon markets, mobility, nature-based solutions, and more. Each section pairs written overviews with podcasts and videos so you can go as deep as you want.

  • Curriculum design
  • Climate tech
  • Self-directed learning
  • Resource curation

04

Travel & community

Constellaciones

A group of friends was traveling across Europe the same summer, different cities, different dates, and there was no good way to see who was where and when. Constellaciones maps everyone's travel schedules so you can spot where you overlap.

It also has a ticket marketplace: if someone can't make it to Movement or Garbicz, they can list their ticket and others in the network can claim it. The best moments happen when you realize you're already in the same city.

  • Coordination tools
  • Community experience design
  • Marketplace UX
  • Event logistics

A few experiences worth naming, not as credentials but as context for how I think about design, operations, and what makes something feel good to be inside of.

Tally

Product and stakeholder operations at a fast-moving startup. Learned how small teams make big decisions, and how much the internal experience shapes what gets built.

Part of one of the most intentionally designed temporary communities in the world. An education in what it looks like when someone actually cares about the experience of being there: design, logistics, and community at a scale most people never touch.

Bachelorette Planning

Designed and ran a full multi-day experience for a group with different energy levels, preferences, and relationships to each other. Equal parts logistics, hospitality, and reading the room.

Ongoing

Events, gatherings, and coordination projects that don't have a formal name. The throughline: caring about how something feels to the people inside it, and doing the work to make it better.
